# UI research: tower-control game

Researched 2026-11-10 against the plan as it stood on that date. The evidence rests on FAA HF-STD-001B (December 2016), FAA alert and situation-awareness studies (1997–2007), ICAO Doc 9830 (2004), EUROCONTROL A-SMGCS specification (2020) and ITWP HMI description (2017), the AAI ATS manual (9 May 2025) and simulator tender (2025–26), and the HCI papers cited below. The requested `../md/designs.md` and `../md/design-research.md` do not exist in this checkout; their color and motion research could not be checked.

## Findings that change the plan

1. **Add an explicit forecast state between clear and conflict.** This changes *B4, “Conflict, incident, and what shows on screen”* and the plan's one-state red alert scheme. ICAO Doc 9830 §2.5.1.1–.7 separates predicted conflict (warning: act soon) from detected/immediate conflict (alarm: immediate action), says alarms take priority, and calls for alerts to remain visible for the conflict. Keep the live red conflict/incident treatment for current events; add a non-red, text-and-shape forecast only when a concrete conflict is predicted, and identify the pair, location, and predicted time. Do not use a generic “30 s” threshold: ICAO's 30 s attention cue applies to its final-approach/touchdown example, not all surface conflicts. [primary]

2. **Make “Tab to next” urgency-aware, with oldest waiting as a tie-breaker.** This changes *Input, item 5* and *B7, “What the page shows”*. Doc 9830 gives runway alerts priority over taxiway, then apron/stand alerts (§3.4.5), and an FAA alert analysis recommends prioritizing alerts by criticality. Neither establishes a universal keyboard queue policy. Use the game’s actionable urgency (imminent predicted conflict / runway blocker / ready clearance) first, then wait age; show why the aircraft is next. This aligns the attention shortcut with hazard priority without claiming direct comparative evidence for FIFO versus priority scheduling. [primary; queue preference remains unverified]

3. **Retain route preview-equals-command, but measure it against the whole task rather than calling one input universally fastest.** This changes *Input, items 1–4*. Card, Moran and Newell's KLM models error-free expert routine performance and reports about 21% RMS prediction error; it does not model learning, error rate, or time-pressure decisions. Under explicit authored operator counts, the key-verb + map-route path is about 15.6 s, mouse-button-heavy routing about 22.7 s, and a hypothetical 58-character typed line about 18.5 s for the same departure. These are model estimates, not observed player results, and the difference largely reflects the assumed number of pointing and mental-preparation operators. A separate command-versus-GUI study found novice GUI tasks faster and less error-prone after brief training, while expert differences were not significant in that small sample; preserve mouse and keyboard routes and validate actual errors in playtests. [primary research]

4. **Keep the forecast and alert cues non-modal, and make them explain cause and affected aircraft.** This changes *B4, “Conflict, incident, and what shows on screen”* and *B7, “The result”*. FAA DOT/FAA/TC-07/22 §1.3.3–1.3.10 says an effective alert should be accurate, new, salient, and should not block ongoing or higher-priority work. A row that says what pair, which rule, measured value and threshold is more actionable than an unexplained red flash; the plan already carries most of this information in incident rows. Preserve that detail in the live forecast and make the event log jump to its timestamp/aircraft on replay. The replay affordance is a recommended design hypothesis; direct evidence that it improves ATC-game outcomes was not found. [primary / authored recommendation]

5. **Treat real strip bays as configurable workflow, not a universal fixed sequence.** This changes *B10 C1 and “Input”*. AAI's 2025–26 simulator tender requires configurable bays, configurable ordering, editable strip fields, and mouse or keyboard edits (sections 2.8.1, pp. 87–89); EUROCONTROL's ITWP HMI uses role/state-filtered valid actions and route preview/accept. These support bays and legal-next actions, but they do not prove the live Delhi HMI uses the same layout. Keep the current phase bays as the game’s understandable workflow, make sort priority visible, and do not present them as a faithful AAI reproduction. [primary]

6. **Keep red reserved, but never make it the only signal; do not copy an operational surface palette as a standard.** This changes *Look and interaction* and the conflict rows in *B4*. W3C WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 requires another cue besides color; NASA's human-factors report also recommends redundant shape, labels, luminance, or texture coding. Red/green distinctions can collapse for protan/deutan viewers, so conflict needs its ring/connector and `CONFLICT` text; incident needs a filled square and `INCIDENT` text. EUROCONTROL documents a particular ITWP green route/red hold-light palette, while EUROCONTROL's A-SMGCS spec says service configuration is locally selected. That is an example, not a universal color convention. The plan’s contrast values cannot be cross-checked because its referenced design research files are missing. [primary]

7. **Use at least 24 CSS px of effective hit target for separate pointer targets; keep the current larger aircraft hit slop and offer a disambiguation path for overlap.** This changes *B10 A1* and the `hit-slop` decision. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 sets 24×24 CSS px minimum subject to spacing/equivalent-control exceptions; SC 2.5.5's enhanced target is 44×44. These are web accessibility criteria, not a claim that aircraft silhouettes should be rendered 24 px wide. The plan's 22 px polygon hit slop is an authored game value; when hit regions overlap, present a stable callsign/phase chooser or cycle candidates, and offer strip selection as an equivalent route. Avoid silently selecting whichever object was drawn last. [primary]

## 1. Real systems and practices

The table is in [real-systems.csv](real-systems.csv). Public evidence does not expose a complete live VIDP electronic-strip HMI: AAI's tender describes a simulator; HOK says the IGI tower integrates radar and electronic flight strips but does not publish the operational screen; the 2016 newspaper account describes paper-strip handoff at that time. NATS, LFV, Indra, and Thales public material did not expose sufficiently detailed field layouts or gestures in this pass. Vendor product claims are not treated as standards.

The transferable rules are workflow-state visibility, valid next actions, configurable organization, explicit accept/send, semantically ranked alerting, timely deactivation, and redundant color-plus-shape/text cues. EUROCONTROL ITWP HMI §3.9.1.1 (20 Jan 2017) provides one specific label example: minimal default data, more information on highlight/request, and a leader line; its 1 px line and 450 px maximum offset are local configuration values, not universal limits. ICAO Doc 9830 does not establish one universal label block or pixel-size minimum. FAA HF-STD-001B §5.6.2 (Dec 2016) uses visual-angle legibility criteria, including 16 arcmin minimum character height and 20–22 arcmin preferred target; those are angular guidance, not browser pixel values, and need a known viewing distance before conversion.

## 2. Games and adjacent command interfaces

The evidence table is in [games.csv](games.csv). Store reviews are individual community reports, not representative samples; their quotes identify concrete interaction failures, not prevalence. Public evidence supports actionable principles (overlap disambiguation, explain alerts, configurable density, input alternatives), but it cannot establish which visual style or input path is fastest for twr. Nothing here recommends copying a game's appearance.

## 3. Input and pointing

The reproducible arithmetic and task sequences are in [klm.md](klm.md). KLM uses `K=.20 s` for a skilled typist, `P=1.10 s` average mouse pointing, `H=.40 s`, `M=1.35 s`, and system response `R` only when the user waits (Card, Moran & Newell 1980, Fig. 1, pp. 397–398). The task's operator counts are authored: the game's precise UI flow is not fully specified as a single typed grammar, and KLM cannot capture route decisions under time pressure. The estimates exclude aircraft movement/sim time, error correction, and novice learning.

For map target acquisition, Fitts's original work establishes a speed/accuracy relation between movement amplitude and target tolerance; it does not prescribe a particular pixel hit-slop. WCAG is the clearest current minimum for DOM controls. For overlapping map targets, expand the target in hit-testing only, then surface a chooser ordered consistently by selection distance and callsign; preserve the aircraft's visible position and use its strip as an alternate selection target. This is a design recommendation, not a measured best-performing technique for tower maps.

Autocomplete should list only legal next tokens, keep the first/default action stable, accept with Tab/Enter, and retain a visible preview before commit. Microsoft's large-scale query-autocomplete study found higher-ranked completions received more engagement and that users often engage after roughly half a query and at word boundaries (Mitra et al., SIGIR 2014). It is not an ATC command study and does not prove ghost text is faster. Do not reorder suggestions while the player is choosing; rank by contextual frequency only when candidates are equally legal, and show why an option is unavailable instead of silently omitting a critical branch.

## 4. Attention, alerts, and workload

Endsley's three SA levels are perception of elements, comprehension of their meaning, and projection of future state. In ATC, the FAA's Endsley/Rodgers report describes awareness of aircraft locations/parameters and projected relative positions, and notes that high workload and attentional narrowing can reduce what controllers notice. Applied to twr: keep callsign/phase/location visible (perception), show the blocking condition in words (comprehension), and show predicted pair/time separately from an active conflict (projection).

Use two alert states, not a ladder of decorative severities: a forecast with a named horizon and a current conflict/incident with a clear response. No source found supports the plan's exact 300 m warning threshold or a universal 30-second preview for surface traffic; those remain authored and should be tuned against false alarms. ICAO Doc 9830 §3.4.5.12–.14 (2004) does specify that its runway protection area be checked 30 seconds before touchdown to raise controller attention, and an alarm issued 15 seconds before touchdown if a target remains; that rule is specific to a landing aircraft and is not a general surface-conflict prediction window. FAA alert research emphasizes accuracy, usefulness, new information, salience, and not disrupting other tasks. ICAO Doc 9830 specifically distinguishes warnings from alarms and prioritizes runway hazards over taxiway and apron hazards.

NASA-TLX measures six subjective dimensions (mental, physical, temporal demand, performance, effort, frustration); FAA ATWIT asks controllers for real-time taskload ratings during simulation. They are evaluation instruments, not runtime player sensors. For a game difficulty proxy, use disclosed state variables such as ready requests, queued aircraft, imminent conflicts, and runway blockers, label it as a game estimate, then calibrate against post-session TLX-style ratings. The current `requesting` indicator and per-bay counts give a foundation.

## 5. Map, labels, and color

The plan's silhouette-plus-minimum-size behavior is reasonable as a readability choice, but no public A-SMGCS source found here establishes the same zoom policy. EUROCONTROL ITWP HMI §3.9.1.1 (2017) supports minimizing default labels, revealing details on highlight/request, and connecting displaced labels to tracks with a leader line; its 450 px offset is a product-specific setting. Keep true scale at close zoom and a minimum-size symbol at overview zoom; show persistent callsigns only when they do not collide, with a leader line and a selected/hovered label as a fallback. The plan's 14 px silhouette floor and 22 px hit slop currently in B10 are authored, not standard values.

For dark map surfaces, retain the design system's existing palette if it passes contrast; separate runway, taxiway, apron and grass by luminance and line pattern as well as hue. Preserve red for current conflicts and incidents as the plan says, but pair it with text and shape. A person with protanopia or deuteranopia may be unable to distinguish red and green; protanomaly can also make red look greener and dimmer (National Eye Institute, “Types of Color Vision Deficiency,” updated 7 Aug 2023). This is why runway/taxiway status and conflict state should not depend on hue alone. No new hex colors are proposed because the house design documents are missing and the task excludes visual restyling.

## 6. Feedback and score

No robust public study was found that establishes whether management-game players generally prefer a blended score or several independent metrics. The plan's separate movements, caused delay, and incidents is therefore a defensible transparency decision, not a universal preference claim. Keep the labels, units, denominators, and tooltips (especially `offered` and game-time delay) beside the values; avoid implying that one raw metric alone measures skill.

The Airport Madness community review below reports delay notifications that contradicted the visible traffic, which is a trust failure in one player report. Preserve causal incident rows with timestamp, rule, aircraft, observed value and limit, and add a replay jump/highlight that shows the event context and the preceding clearance. The benefit of causal replay is a design hypothesis; a controlled game study was not located.

## Gaps

- No public live VIDP EFS image/spec with real strip fields, bay order, or gestures; AAI tender is for an ATS training simulator. No detailed public NATS, LFV, Indra, or Thales production HMI was located.
- No authoritative universal A-SMGCS label schema, clutter/leader-line rule, or browser-pixel minimum was found. Doc 9830 is not a UI style guide.
- No direct study comparing FIFO versus urgency-first strip selection, or testing autocomplete/ghost text in tower-control phraseology under time pressure.
- No representative study found on blended versus multimetric scores or causal replay in ATC games.
- Requested house design references are missing at `../md/designs.md` and `../md/design-research.md`; existing contrast values therefore remain unverified in this run.
- BeyondATC and some commercial ATC interfaces are either dynamically presented or lack enough accessible, sourceable UI detail in this pass. Community complaints here are anecdotes.
